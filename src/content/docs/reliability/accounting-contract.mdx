An Accounting-Request is a record a NAS wants to hand over: a session started, is still running, or ended. This page
says exactly what radiusward promises when it answers — and when it stays silent. It is the property radiusward is
built around.

## The guarantee

<Callout type="tip" title="When radiusward sends an Accounting-Response">
The record has been appended to the replicated log and **written to disk with fsync on a majority of the cluster**
(two of three members). It survives the loss of any single member, including a power loss of that member.
</Callout>

The RADIUS server sends the response only after the cluster reports the record **committed**. Not after it was queued
locally, not after a network send succeeded, and not after a single member received it.

What the guarantee assumes:

- **Three cluster members**, a majority of which can reach each other.
- **fsync on** — `archive.file.sync.level=2`, the default: data and metadata are flushed to disk before a member
  confirms. Below that level a power loss can lose acknowledged records; the lower levels exist for tests, not for
  production.
- **Disks that honour fsync.** On Linux this is tested with a simulated power loss of all members
  (<a href={`${REPORTS}/linux/`}>fault-injection report</a>). macOS `fsync` does not flush the drive cache, so
  durability is claimed for Linux only.

## What an acknowledgement does not mean

**It does not mean "written to the billing database".** Databases and other destinations (sinks) are fed from the
committed log *after* the acknowledgement, normally within milliseconds to seconds. Committed in the log and delivered
to a sink are two different things, and radiusward keeps them apart:

- While a sink is down, the log on disk is the buffer and acknowledgements continue.
- A sink marked `required` that falls more than `sink.<name>.max.lag` behind (default 4 hours) **stops new
  acknowledgements** until it catches up — the NAS keeps the records and retries.
- An optional sink is detached instead and can be repaired later with `radiusward admin resync`, as long as the log
  still holds the position.
- In the database, delivery is exactly once: events are inserted only if absent, sessions updated only if newer, and a
  replaced exporter cannot write.

`radiusward admin verify-export` compares a log of acknowledged records with the database and the cluster.

## When radiusward stays silent

RADIUS has no "Accounting-Reject". When radiusward cannot promise the record is safe, it does not answer, and the NAS
retransmits — which NAS devices do by design. It stays silent when:

- the packet is malformed, comes from an address that is not a configured NAS, or its authenticator does not verify;
- a RADIUS server's queue is full (it sheds load instead of blocking);
- **no majority of the cluster is reachable**, or a leader election is in progress;
- writing to disk fails;
- a required sink is further behind than its limit.

<Callout type="note" title="Silence is not loss">
Silence never means a record was lost *after* an acknowledgement. It means the NAS still owns the record and must
retry or buffer it. Losing two of three members at once is the case the cluster cannot cover: it stops acknowledging
until a majority is back — consistency before availability, on purpose.
</Callout>

## Retransmissions and duplicates

A NAS that got no answer cannot know whether its record was committed, so it retries — possibly after a leader
failover, and possibly through the other RADIUS server. radiusward recognises a retry at two levels:

| Level | Recognises | By |
|---|---|---|
| RADIUS server response cache | an identical retransmission to the same RADIUS server | source address and port, RADIUS Identifier, Request Authenticator |
| Cluster | the same accounting event, whichever way it arrives | NAS identity, `Acct-Session-Id`, `Acct-Status-Type`, and `Acct-Session-Time` or `Event-Timestamp` |

A duplicate is answered with an Accounting-Response like the original and is **not applied or exported a second
time**. The cluster's key deliberately leaves out `Acct-Delay-Time` (it changes on every retransmission) and the
8-bit Identifier (it is reused).

How long duplicates are recognised:

- **While the session lives**, and for `service.tombstone` after its Stop (default 1 hour): Interim updates that are
  not newer than the last one applied are duplicates.
- Events without a progress value are remembered by their exact key for `service.exact.key.window` (default
  15 minutes). A retransmission older than that window could be applied twice; sinks are idempotent per event as a
  second line of defence.
- The deduplication state is part of the cluster's snapshots and survives restarts.

Records that arrive out of order are handled too: an Interim whose Start never arrived creates the session, a late
Start is merged into it, and records arriving after the Stop are acknowledged and kept as stale rather than reopening
the session.

## What the cluster tolerates

<Cards>
	<Card title="Tolerated without losing acknowledged records">
		One cluster member lost (crash, power loss, disk loss) · any number of RADIUS servers lost · network partitions that
		leave a majority connected · sink outages up to their lag limit.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Not tolerated">
		Two of three members lost at once: no majority, so no acknowledgements until one returns. The surviving
		member's data is intact. A continuous backup outside the cluster restores a cluster that lost its quorum, up to
		the backup's position.
	</Card>
</Cards>

## Questions for your evaluation

| Situation | Check before production |
|---|---|
| Leader failure | how long your NAS waits before retrying, and that its retries are counted once |
| Loss of the majority | how long your NAS buffers accounting, and which of your authentication features need the cluster |
| A sink that is down | that lag is visible and alerted, and whether the sink is required or optional |
| A lost quorum | that the continuous backup runs, how far behind it is, and that a restore has been practised |

## The evidence

Every statement on this page maps to an automated test in the{' '}
<a href={`${DOCS}/testing/failure-matrix.md`}>failure matrix</a> — for example: no acknowledgement without a
majority, and the retry applied once after recovery; the leader killed under load with every acknowledged record
kept; a RADIUS server killed between commit and response, with the retry through the other RADIUS server applied once. The
results are published as <a href={`${REPORTS}/`}>test reports</a>: fault injection on Linux nightly (power loss with
LazyFS, partitions, full disks), the Kubernetes scenarios nightly, end-to-end and integration tests on every change.
