What a NAS and an operator see when part of radiusward fails. Each scenario states what to expect, what it assumes,
how it recovers, where it ends, and which test checks it. The rule behind all of them is the
[accounting contract](/docs/reliability/accounting-contract/): **nothing is acknowledged before it is safe on a
majority, and nothing acknowledged is lost while a majority survives.**

<Callout type="note" title="Two kinds of pause">
A NAS waits briefly during a leader election, and indefinitely while no majority exists — in both cases it keeps the
records and retries, which is what RADIUS clients are built to do. What it never sees is an acknowledgement for a
record that is later lost.
</Callout>

The scenarios: [the leader is killed](#leader) · [a gateway fails](#gateway) · [the majority is lost](#majority) ·
[a sink is down](#sink) · [a server loses its disk](#disk) · [two servers are lost](#quorum).

## Not covered

- **Two members lost at once with their disks**, and no backup: the cluster cannot be restored. Run the continuous
  backup.
- **Storage that ignores fsync** (some virtual disks, or caches without power-loss protection) voids the durability
  guarantee; it is tested on Linux, not on macOS.
- **Members on high-latency links** do not lose data, but every acknowledgement waits for the round trip.
