The Helm chart runs the whole cluster — three members, the RADIUS servers, the backup — and is tested nightly on kind under
load while the leader is killed, members and RADIUS servers are rolled, a volume is replaced and a lost quorum is restored.

## Install

```bash
helm install radiusward oci://ghcr.io/radiusward/charts/radiusward --version 0.1.0-rc.1 \
    -n radius --create-namespace -f my-values.yaml
kubectl -n radius get pods -l app.kubernetes.io/instance=radiusward
kubectl -n radius exec radiusward-node-0 -- radiusward probe http://127.0.0.1:9090/ready
```

A minimal `my-values.yaml`:

```yaml
profile: small                      # small | standard | large
node:
  persistence: {size: 100Gi, storageClass: local-ssd}
  export:
    postgres: {enabled: true, url: "jdbc:postgresql://postgres:5432/radius", existingSecret: radius-db}
server:
  nas:
    - {name: branch-routers, address: 10.20.0.0/16, existingSecret: nas-secrets, existingSecretKey: branch, vendor: cisco}
  auth:
    enabled: true
    stores:
      - {name: db, type: sql, url: "jdbc:postgresql://postgres:5432/radius", user: radius, existingSecret: radius-db}
    eap:
      methods: [peap, ttls]
      tls: {existingSecret: radius-eap-tls}     # a kubernetes.io/tls Secret, plus ca.crt for EAP-TLS
```

Secrets never go into ConfigMaps: NAS secrets and store passwords come from Kubernetes Secrets as environment
variables, users files and certificates are mounted from Secrets. Every configuration key is reachable through
`node.config` and `server.config`.

## What the chart creates

| Object | Purpose |
|---|---|
| StatefulSet `<release>-node`, 3 pods | the members; each finds the others by DNS name and has one volume |
| headless Service `<release>-node` | the members' names, published before they are ready (none is ready without a quorum) |
| StatefulSet `<release>-server`, 2 pods by default | the RADIUS servers; no volumes |
| Service `<release>-server-<n>` per RADIUS server | its own address for the NAS devices — a LoadBalancer with `externalTrafficPolicy: Local` |
| StatefulSet `<release>-backup` | the continuous backup, with its own volume |
| PodDisruptionBudgets | members `maxUnavailable: 1`, RADIUS servers `minAvailable: 1` |
| NetworkPolicies (optional) | Aeron only between members and RADIUS servers; RADIUS from the networks you list |
| PodMonitor, PrometheusRule, dashboard ConfigMaps (optional) | the alert rules and the five Grafana dashboards |

## Two addresses per NAS

Configure every NAS with **two RADIUS servers**, as its primary and secondary server. When a node fails, the NAS
retries through the other RADIUS server and the cluster recognises the retry. With a single shared address, Kubernetes
keeps sending part of the traffic to a failed node's RADIUS server until it marks the node NotReady — about 40–50 seconds;
in the project's own test that lost 7.6 % of the accounting, where two addresses lost nothing.

NAS entries are matched by source address, so the RADIUS servers must see it: `externalTrafficPolicy: Local` on cloud
load balancers and MetalLB (the chart's default), or RADIUS servers on edge nodes with host networking.

## What the nodes need

- **Larger UDP socket buffers** than most kernels allow by default; the sysctls are not namespaced, so set them on
  the nodes: `net.core.rmem_max=2097152 net.core.wmem_max=2097152`.
- **Storage that honours fsync and answers quickly**: every Accounting-Response waits for an fsync on a majority.
  Local volumes are best; network block storage works with higher latency. 100 Gi per member is ample for 500,000
  sessions.
- **Guaranteed CPU** for the members (requests equal limits in the chart): a throttled leader looks dead and costs an
  election.

| Profile | Member | RADIUS server |
|---|---|---|
| small | 1 CPU, 1.5 Gi | 1 CPU, 768 Mi |
| standard | 2 CPU, 4 Gi | 2 CPU, 1.25 Gi |
| large | 4 CPU, 11 Gi | 4 CPU, 2.25 Gi |

## Replacing a member's volume

Delete the member's claim and pod. The new pod finds its volume empty, copies the latest snapshot and the log after
it from the other members, and rejoins — on kind this took 4 seconds under load, and the member was ready 10 seconds
after it started. The other two keep the quorum meanwhile.

## A lost quorum

The chart runs the continuous backup. After a lost quorum, `scripts/k8s-restore.sh -n <namespace> -r <release>`
restores all three members from it through temporary pods that mount one volume at a time, so node-local volumes work
too. The cluster goes on from the backup's position.

## Upgrades

`helm upgrade` rolls the members one at a time; a restarted member counts as ready only once its election is closed,
so the quorum is kept. Each member restart may cost one leader election of about 2 seconds, within a NAS's
retransmission time — on kind, rolling every member under load lost nothing and caused no retransmission. RADIUS servers
roll one at a time as well; a stopping RADIUS server keeps answering for `server.shutdownDrain` (5 s) after it reports
not ready, but a NAS that sent to it in that moment retries through its secondary. Restart RADIUS servers in quiet hours if
NAS timeouts matter.

Changes to how records are applied activate on their own once every member runs the new version; after that, an
image older than the active state version is refused.
