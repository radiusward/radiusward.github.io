radiusward is a RADIUS server — the service that network equipment asks whether a user or device may connect
(authentication) and tells about the sessions it carries (accounting). Access points, switches, broadband gateways,
VPN concentrators and mobile core functions all speak RADIUS. Their name in RADIUS is **NAS**, network access server.

What sets radiusward apart is what happens to accounting.

## Accounting that survives a node

A NAS reports every session with Accounting-Requests: a Start, Interim updates while it runs, a Stop at the end.
Those records are what billing, usage limits and audits are built on. radiusward answers such a request only after
the record has been **written to disk on two of three cluster members**. If any one member crashes — even loses
power — nothing that was acknowledged is lost, and the NAS, which retries until it gets an answer, never has to be
told twice. Retries that arrive after a failover, or through the other gateway, are recognised and counted once.

The exact promise, its assumptions and its limits are in [the accounting contract](/docs/reliability/accounting-contract/).

## Authentication on the gateways

Access-Requests are decided by the **gateways**, the processes that hold the network sockets, from identity stores
you already have: a users file, SQL, LDAP or Active Directory, or Active Directory over Netlogon. radiusward speaks
PAP, CHAP, MS-CHAP and the EAP methods used for Wi-Fi and wired 802.1X — EAP-TLS, PEAP, TTLS, TEAP, EAP-pwd — and
EAP-SIM, EAP-AKA and EAP-AKA′ for SIM cards. Only the state that every gateway must agree on — how many devices a user
has online, SIM sequence numbers — goes through the cluster.

It carries RADIUS over UDP, TLS (RadSec), DTLS and TCP; forwards realms to other RADIUS servers (proxying); and sends
Change-of-Authorization and Disconnect requests to the NAS.

## How it runs

<Cards>
	<Card title="Three cluster members">
		Hold the replicated accounting log and its state, built on Aeron Cluster with Raft consensus. Each also exports
		the committed records to PostgreSQL, JSON-lines files or other RADIUS servers.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Two or more gateways">
		Hold the sockets, check every packet, decide authentication, forward accounting to the cluster and answer after
		the commit. They keep no state of their own that matters.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Where it runs">
		A container image for amd64 and arm64; a Helm chart for Kubernetes; systemd units for hosts; example templates for
		Amazon ECS. Java 25.
	</Card>
</Cards>

## Who it is for

radiusward is for operators whose accounting records matter — internet providers, campus and enterprise networks,
private mobile networks — and who want to read what a RADIUS server guarantees, and check it, before relying on it.
[Is it a fit?](/docs/start/fit/) lists when it is, and when something simpler serves better.

## Status

radiusward is open source under the Apache-2.0 licence and young: the first release candidate, `v0.1.0-rc.1`, came out
in November 2026. Its behaviour under failures is tested automatically and published; it does not yet have a
production track record, device certifications or an independent security audit.
